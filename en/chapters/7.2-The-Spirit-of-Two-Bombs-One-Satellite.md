# 7.2 The Spirit of "Two Bombs, One Satellite"

## In 1960, the Soviets left

In July 1960, Sino-Soviet relations deteriorated to a critical point. In 1959, the Soviet Union refused to provide nuclear technical assistance to China. Khrushchev issued an order, and the Soviet Union evacuated all experts in China overnight. 1,390 experts were evacuated from nuclear weapons research institutes, missile design institutes, steel plants, and mines at the same time. All the drawings were taken away, all the technical information was taken away, the centrifuge of the uranium enrichment plant under construction was dismantled in half, and the installation guide was stuffed into a box and shipped on the train to Moscow.

Oxygen has been cut off for China's nuclear weapons and missile research and development projects that are just starting up.

Many years later, Deng Jiaxian recalled this incident and said:

**"When the Soviets left, they told us: Without us, you would never be able to build an atomic bomb in your lifetime." **

This is not a praising statement, it is a recognized "fact" at the time. China had no mainframe computers, just a few M-3 tube computers left over from the Soviet Union, which operated about 30 operations per second. Change to a unit you're familiar with: 30 FLOPS. Any Cortex-M0 you have on hand is far more than this number. Without precision machine tools, manufacturing centrifuge rotors required micron-level tolerances. At that time, the best grinding machines in the country could only achieve wire-level (10 μm) accuracy. There is not enough uranium ore, and geological exploration teams are still looking for ore across the country. There is no engineering team with complete training in nuclear physics. Many young people involved in the project were directly recruited from university physics departments and graduated early. On the global technological map in 1959, China was short of almost everything.

But there are some things that cannot be done just because the conditions are not met. National security cannot be synonymous with “building after resources are in place.”

What happened next?

On October 16, 1964, 5 years from scratch, the first atomic bomb exploded in Lop Nur.
On June 17, 1967, 3 years later, the first hydrogen bomb exploded. From the atomic bomb to the hydrogen bomb, it only took China 2 years and 8 months. The same leap took the United States 7 years and 3 months and the Soviet Union 4 years. China's speed is the fastest in mankind.
On April 24, 1970, three years later, the first artificial satellite "Dongfanghong-1" was launched.

From the zero foundation to the full success of nuclear bombs + missiles + satellites, it took a little more than 10 years. In the past ten years, the "equipment environment" faced by participants is almost equivalent to a blank by today's standards.

How? This is not a "spiritual victory method." This involves specific and quantitative engineering operations.

## Three people, three choices

**Deng Jiaxian: 28 years. **

In 1950, at the age of 26, Deng Jiaxian received a PhD in physics from Purdue University. On the 9th day he boarded the ship returning home. In 1958, Qian Sanqiang came to him and said: "The country is going to hold a 'big firecracker' and I would like to invite you to participate." Deng Jiaxian went home and said to his wife Xu Luxi: "I want to mobilize my work. From now on, my life will be handed over to this matter. If it is done, it will be worth dying."

In the 28 years since then, Deng Jiaxian’s name has completely disappeared from the international physics community. His wife had no idea what he was doing or where he was. Until 1985, Deng Jiaxian was admitted to the hospital due to terminal rectal cancer. The cause of the cancer was the failure of the warhead to explode during a nuclear test in 1979. As the person in charge of theoretical design, Deng Jiaxian personally entered the blast core area to check the unexploded warheads. He wore protective clothing, but radioactive contamination could not be completely isolated.

Xu Luxi held his hand in front of his hospital bed, which had been severely ulcerated due to radiation burns. Deng Jiaxian's whole body's hematopoietic function was destroyed by radiation, and all the doctors could do was relieve the pain. Deng Jiaxian passed away on July 29, 1986 at the age of 62. His last request before his death was: "Don't let people leave us too far behind."


**Wang Ganchang: Seventeen years, a fake name. **

In December 1960, a nuclear physicist who was already world-famous returned from the Joint Institute for Nuclear Research in Dubna, Soviet Union. Previously, he led the team to discover the anti-sigma negative hyperon, which was recognized as a major breakthrough in the global physics community at that time. The academic community generally believed that he was only one step away from the Nobel Prize. Had he stayed in the Soviet Union or gone to Europe and America, he would have continued to be at the forefront of first-class science.In 1961, he walked into an office in Beijing and accepted an assignment. Since then, his name has disappeared from all international academic journals.

His name is **Wang Ganchang**. For the next seventeen years, he only used the pseudonym "Wang Jing".

His children have no idea where their father is or what he is doing. His wife can only contact him through a mailbox number. The children asked their mother, "Where is dad?" and the mother said, "Dad is in the mailbox." One year, Wang Ganchang's youngest daughter saw a report in the newspaper "Comrade Wang Ganchang." She asked her mother, "Who is this Wang Ganchang? Is he his father?" The mother looked at it and said, "No. Dad's name is Wang Jing."

Seventeen years is the entire growth cycle from the time a child enters elementary school to graduating from college. The identity of the father only exists in a mailbox number. Wang Ganchang later wrote: "I am willing to dedicate myself to the country."

**Guo Yonghuai: The last moment of life. **

On December 5, 1968, a plane crashed approaching Beijing Airport.

Guo Yonghuai worked continuously at the Qinghai nuclear weapons development base for more than two months. He discovered a key piece of data that needed to be brought back to Beijing immediately for verification. The base leader advised him to take the train for safety. After Guo Yonghuai arrived in Lanzhou after taking the train for more than ten hours, he heard that there was a convenient flight to Beijing that night and immediately changed his ticket. He can't wait, and the data can't wait.

The plane stalled and crashed just 400 meters from the Beijing airport runway. Search and rescue personnel found two bodies hugging each other in the wreckage. When people separated the two bodies with force, they were stunned: there was an intact briefcase sandwiched between the two chests. Inside is key data from thermonuclear weapons experiments.

One of them is named Guo Yonghuai. The world's top aerodynamicist and professor at Cornell University. In 1956, on the eve of his return to China, he burned his manuscripts that had not been published for many years in front of his American colleagues. He said: "These things belong to human knowledge. But before I return to China, I cannot let them fall into the hands of the US government." Another person is his guard Mou Fangdong. At the end of their lives, they used their bodies to make a fireproof safe.

Twenty-two days later, on December 27, 1968, China successfully conducted a thermonuclear weapon test (China’s first hydrogen bomb test was successful on June 17, 1967). Guo Yonghuai didn't see it, but in the dozen seconds when the plane crashed, he used his last consciousness to protect what he had to protect. Not his life, but data.

In 2018, the International Minor Planet Center named an asteroid "Guo Yonghuaixing".

This is not an isolated case. At that time, almost all the top physicists in China: Deng Jiaxian, Qian Sanqiang, Yu Min, Zhu Guangya, Cheng Kaijia, Chen Nengkuan, and Zhou Guangzhao were all making the same choice in different ways. Many of them could have lived a superior life abroad, done cutting-edge research, and published top-notch papers. But they're back. What I faced when I came back was not a first-class laboratory, but an abacus, a hand-cranked computer, and a tent on the Gobi Desert.

## Abacus and hand-cranked computer: implosion structures figured out

The **implosion structure** of the atomic bomb is the core problem. The principle sounds simple: use the detonation wave of high-energy chemical explosives to compress the subcritical fissile material (plutonium-239 or uranium-235) uniformly from all sides, increase the density to supercritical, and then the chain reaction starts. But the difficulty of engineering implementation is suffocating.

Explosive Lens: This is no ordinary block of explosives. It is composed of two alternating layers of explosives with different detonation speeds, fast and slow. The design goal is to allow the detonation waves emitted from various points on the spherical detonation surface to reach the center at the same time. If the arrival time deviation at any point exceeds tens of nanoseconds, the compression will no longer be spherically symmetrical, and the plutonium ball will be "pinched" into an irregular shape. The chain reaction may not start at all, or it may be locally supercritical and then the material will scatter, and the explosion power will be greatly reduced.

To design the geometry of an explosive lens, you need to solve more than simple geometry problems. You need to calculate the propagation and Mach reflection of detonation waves in curved layered media, you need to calculate the nonlinear state equation of materials under millions of atmospheres, and you need to calculate the multiplication and reflection of neutrons in supercritical systems. Each involves extremely computationally intensive partial differential equations.

What to use?

At that time, there were only a few tube computers left over from the Soviet Union in the country. The calculation speed is approximately 30 operations per second. Not nearly enough. Massive calculations were distributed to universities and research institutes across the country, and were completed using **hand-cranked computers + abacus**.How to calculate it specifically? It's not just a few random abacus beads. They use numerical methods with a rigorous algorithmic framework.

**Successive Over-Relaxation (SOR)**: A standard iterative method for solving large sparse linear equations. With thousands of grid points, each iteration is divided into four groups for parallel advancement: the first group is responsible for the current iteration estimate, the second group calculates the residuals, the third group does super-relaxed extrapolation (multiplying the residuals by a relaxation factor greater than 1 to accelerate convergence), and the fourth group does proofreading. The rhythm of the abacus beads is organized into a human-powered numerical calculation assembly line. If one grid point is calculated incorrectly, the residuals of the entire iteration round will be skewed. The proofreading team can find anomalies in the summarized data and trace back to the erroneous group and location. This is to use organizational management to make up for the unreliability of single-point calculations, which is exactly the same idea as checksum + retry in today's distributed systems.

**Dimensional analysis**: Use the π theorem to simplify complex partial differential equations involving seven or eight physical parameters into simple relationships involving only two or three dimensionless numbers. For example: the propagation of shock waves depends on explosion energy, distance, time, initial air density, and initial air pressure, a total of five dimensional parameters. The π theorem tells you that these five parameters actually only determine two dimensionless numbers: dimensionless distance and dimensionless time. You compressed the search space of five variables to two, and compressed the parameter space from five dimensions to two dimensions. The computational effort is reduced by more than an order of magnitude. This is not "being lazy", this is using physical intuition to do mathematical dimensionality reduction. It's essentially the same as using a LUT (lookup table) to replace floating-point polynomials in an embedded system: using physical relationships for dimensionality reduction and compression under limited resources.

**Original form of the Monte Carlo method**: Instead of computer-generated pseudo-random numbers, physical random sources (rolling dice, drawing lots) are used to generate sampling points, and these sampling points are used to obtain an integral approximation of the neutron transport equation. The efficiency is extremely low, but the accuracy is controllable. As long as you roll the dice enough times, the statistical error will converge to an acceptable range. They simulated thousands of neutron collisions by rolling dice and drawing lots. The scattering angle, energy loss, and absorption probability of each collision were all manually recorded on long rolls of paper. A piece of paper is tens of meters long when opened, and it is densely packed with numbers. This is China's first "Monte Carlo simulation engine". The running medium is not silicon-based transistors, but carbon-based neurons.

The Institute of Computing Technology of the Chinese Academy of Sciences in Beijing writes calculation forms on large rolls of paper and calculates values ​​one by one. Every time the laboratory updates a parameter, for example, if the measured detonation velocity of a certain batch of explosives is 0.3% higher than the nominal value, all values ​​will be recalculated for half a month. Thousands of points, countless iterations, pure handwork. If you make a mistake in any line, start over from the beginning.

**Their calculation tools are thousands of times worse than what you have available on any debugging platform today, but the calculation results are not wrong. **

Imagine you are using command line gdb to debug a tricky CAN FD message parsing logic. You complain that the tool isn't intuitive enough. Now imagine: you are trying to solve a nonlinear partial differential equation, and your tools are an abacus and a pencil. One material parameter is updated and all are recalculated for half a month.

Your gdb is still there, it's just waiting for you to press the next c.

## Hand polishing: What replaces precision machine tools is time and body

Uranium enrichment requires centrifuges. The centrifuge rotor rotates at a speed of tens of thousands of revolutions per minute. At this speed, the centrifugal force on the outer wall of the rotor is up to hundreds of thousands of times its own weight. Any slight mass asymmetry, even if the rotor diameter deviation exceeds 1 μm, will produce violent periodic vibrations that will tear the rotor bearings apart within a few seconds. The flying fragments shot through the centrifuge casing like bullets.

Precision machine tools? No. Soviet experts took away the drawings.

According to the memories of participants at that time, workers from a manufacturing plant in Shanghai took over this task. There is no CNC grinding machine, no laser automatic measurement system, and no constant temperature and humidity factory building. **They spent several months polishing it by hand and repeatedly measuring it with an optical collimator to create a rotor that can turn tens of thousands of revolutions per minute without chattering. ** A master takes a fine oilstone and rubs it in circles on the surface of the rotor every day. After each round of grinding, rest the rotor on the V-block and use a dial indicator to look for runout. If it exceeds the tolerance, continue grinding; if it falls below the tolerance, scrap it and start over. Repeat hundreds, thousands of times until the tolerance stabilizes at the sub-micron level.

He doesn't have high-precision machine tools. But **he replaced the machine tool with time and patience. **This is not "hard work." This is **engineering methodology**: use the minimum viable tool to achieve target accuracy, and replace automated closed loops with human closed loops. In essence, it is exactly the same methodology as when you debug a PCB without a logic analyzer and use an oscilloscope to manually decode the SPI waveform frame by frame: measure → correct → measure again → correct again, forming a closed loop. It's just that what he is facing is a micron-level rotor, and what you are facing is microsecond-level timing. The frequency of the closed loop is different. His cycle is "one polishing → one measurement" for about 10 minutes, and your cycle is "one programming → one waveform observation" for about 5 minutes, but the essence of the closed loop is exactly the same.

## White shirt: Use personal risk to guarantee collective safety

Nuclear weapons testing is no joke. One calculation error and one operational error can cost dozens of lives and irreversible environmental pollution.

There is a general who is responsible for on-site command. He will do one thing before each experiment: wear only a white shirt, walk to the forefront of the experimental site, and walk around. Make sure all safety measures are taken and every evacuation checkpoint is in place. Confirm that the detonation cable is connected, confirm the trigger settings of the seismometer, and confirm that all personnel are in their designated positions in the bunker. Then he returned to the command post, picked up the microphone, and ordered the detonation.

Why should he go in person? He can send a staff officer there and read the report at the headquarters. But he doesn't. **He uses personal risks to ensure collective security. This is a sense of responsibility that cannot be quantified. ** If you are responsible for the wiring of explosives at the scene and see the general walking in front of you, his white shirt fluttering in the wind of the Gobi Desert, you will feel at ease. You don't report to him in a hierarchy, but you are willing to do your best for him.

There are no KPI assessments, no performance appraisal bonuses, and no "signature of the person responsible for safety." He just went. Because he is in charge, he is present, and he knows what is going on. You can see this sense of responsibility reflected in your best technical managers. He does the last review before releasing the version late at night, is the first to enter the conference room when there is a production accident, and is the first to find the root cause when the production line is shut down. The engineering community needs such people on site.

## Mura’s Arc – From Human Eyes to Physical Root Causes

I experienced a similar feeling when I was working as a yield engineer in a panel factory. Although the scale and weight are not the same, the sense of urgency of "doing everything to find the answer" is the same.

Once, when I was sampling a grayscale image, I noticed a faint arc of Mura.

Mura is uneven brightness on the display panel. AOI can detect particles and scratches, but not Mura. The judgment of Mura basically depends on the human eye. The Mura I saw was very light, a small arc.

I traced back the batches before and after, and the Mura became lighter and shallower toward the front, and was completely absent before a certain point in time. **Time range is locked. **

I observed the spatial distribution: On a large panel, only a few adjacent edges of fixed positions have this Mura, and these positions are spliced together to look like a ring.

**Ring distribution → Ring mechanism contact**. This is the first step in physical intuition.

There are hundreds of processes and hundreds of machines in a panel factory. Based on the ring phenomenon, I eliminated equipment without ring structures (cleaning machines, wet etching machines) and narrowed it down to equipment with ring contacts.

Then find the point diagrams of these devices: the contact positions of the robotic arm or load-bearing mechanism. Compare the arc position of Mura with the point bitmap. Corresponding to three pieces of equipment: the pickup and delivery robotic arm of the yellow light machine, the carrying mechanism of the CVD machine, and the grabbing mechanism of the ion implantation equipment.

Next is the elimination method: pull the panel that has only passed through the CVD machine, no Mura is found, and the CVD is eliminated.

There are only two ion implantation equipment, and one of them happens to be under maintenance, and all panels go through the same one. The panels of the yellow light machine must also have passed through this ion implantation machine. Both devices were suspect, and the clues were broken.

**The second step of physical intuition: production sequence. **

It suddenly occurred to me: the panels in the same Cassette, the higher the mura, the deeper. The further back in time, the more severe the mura. This shows that the panels in Cassette are produced from bottom to top in the abnormal machine.

I checked the production order of the two devices:
- Yellow light machine: from bottom to top
- Ion implantation machine: from top to bottom (opposite direction)

**The direction is right. Lock: yellow light machine. **Equipment engineers inspected the pick-and-delivery arm and discovered that a gasket contacting the panel had deteriorated. The aging of the gasket causes abnormal contact pressure, leaving regular marks on the panel surface, which eventually manifests as arc-shaped Mura.

Replacing the gasket increased the yield by 0.3%. Problem solved.

It took a day and a half from the time Mura was discovered to the time the device was locked. There are only two core troubleshooting ideas: **annular distribution → ring mechanism** (physical intuition); **Cassette production sequence → equipment direction** (physical intuition).

## Your own "two bombs and one satellite" moment

You may think: "Two bombs and one satellite" is a grand narrative, what does it have to do with me?

There is a relationship. The "insufficient resources" you encounter in automotive electronics engineering are your Gobi Desert:

- Don't have a logic analyzer? Manually decode the SPI waveform with an oscilloscope and view it frame by frame. Frame by frame. Take a day.
- No simulation environment? Burn the code into Flash and try again and again. A hundred reboots. Two hundred restarts. Until the timing is right.
- No external RAM? Fine allocation in 128KB SRAM: CAN buffer, UDS frame buffer, calibration data buffer, DTC recording area, the starting address of each piece has been calculated, and no byte can be wasted between adjacent blocks.
- No dedicated security chip? Use MCU internal CSEc + MPU + ECC verification to create a "HSM-like" solution. Not a true HSM, but the security level is adequate and the cost of attack is high enough.
- 32KB Flash is not enough to store calibration data? Segmented compression + differential storage + wear leveling, "steal" margin from limited space.
- Is the AUTOSAR configuration tool license too expensive? Use the free ARXML editor to handwrite the configuration and check it line by line. Slow, but error-free.
- There is only one sample. If it breaks, it will be gone? Before clamping the oscilloscope probe on, think three times, confirm three times, and take a deep breath, just like walking into an experimental site wearing a white shirt. Because you know, if it is broken, it will be gone. 

**Every time you complete a seemingly impossible task with limited resources, you are writing your own "two bombs and one satellite." **

The abacus and hand-crank computer of our predecessors are your oscilloscope and command line debugger. Your constraints are no less than theirs (time, computing power, non-negotiable security), but you still made it happen. You put the calibration data + Bootloader + application program + CRC check area in the 32KB Flash, and you still need to leave an erase margin, and you did it. This is not a trick, this is engineering.

## "Two bombs and one satellite" is not history - it is an attitude towards life

The spirit of "two bombs and one satellite" is often simplified into four words: "hard work". But this simplification is problematic. It can easily lead to the misunderstanding that "the poorer, the more glorious". Eating coarse grains, wearing patched clothes, and staying up late and working overtime are not the spirit itself. **The spirit itself is: "Under given limited conditions, use human initiative to create results that transcend conditional limitations." **

If mainframe computers had been available at the time, they would have used them. If there is a precision CNC machine, they will use it. If there are foreign experts, they will learn with humility. But not then. **So they took a harder road and came down because they didn't give up. **

When faced with the thousands of security requirements of ISO 26262 ASIL-D, you choose to bite them off one by one instead of hoping that "customers will lower the security level."
When you face the debugging nightmare caused by AUTOSAR's multi-layer abstraction, choose to understand the timing semantics of each layer instead of complaining that "the software is too heavy."
When you are faced with an oscilloscope, a sample, and a 3,000-page reference manual, and want to run through the entire motor control algorithm, you choose to get started.

Every "seemingly impossible" project you take on now is your personal "two bombs and one star." The engineer who frowned around a sensor in the Gobi Desert in 1964, Deng Jiaxian who wore protective clothing and walked into the nuclear explosion center to check for unexploded bombs in 1979, and the equipment engineer who was still wearing dust-free clothing and dismantling polishing pads in front of the CMP machine at three o'clock this morning are the same type of people as you sitting in front of the oscilloscope today.You are not "suffering". Eating instant noodles is not spiritual, and staying up late is not spiritual. The spirit is that you know that "the conditions are not enough", but you do not take "the conditions are not enough" as a reason to give up. You chose **find a way**. You use differential to cancel the noise, use oversampling to purify the signal, use Kalman filtering to fuse multiple sensors, and use calibration transfer to move the accuracy from the metrology institute to the production line.

**You are using your subjective initiative to fight against eternal resource boundaries. ** This is "two bombs and one satellite". From 1964 to today, from the tent in Lop Nur to the centrifuge workshop in Shanghai, from Bell Labs in New Jersey to the "embedded development" board in Shenzhen, from the satellite in the sky to the circuit board in your hand, the same spirit continues.

One day you are old. You're sitting on the couch, and your child asks you, "Mom, Dad, what did you do when you were young?" You say, "I built things on cars. Tiny, tiny things: programs that run on a chip." And you might be silent for a while. You don't know how to explain it. But you know, those nights when you sit in front of the oscilloscope, the weight of your finger on the probe; those afternoons when you move the address offset inch by inch in the 128KB SRAM; those early mornings when you want to give up when faced with a bug, and then take a deep breath to continue debugging. Together, they truly define your life more than any words can.

You are not doing "embedded development". You're doing something much bigger than that. **You are continuing a human spirit: don’t give up under not good enough conditions, find a way, make results, and then record them and pass them on to the next person. **
